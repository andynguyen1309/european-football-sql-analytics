# Theme 3 — Market Value and Player Development

## Business objective

Understand how player market values developed across Europe’s top five domestic leagues, how growth differed by age and position, and which clubs were most effective at generating valuable talent.

## Analytical scope

- **Competitions:** Premier League, La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A and Ligue 1
- **Seasons:** 2020–21 to 2024–25
- **Valuation window:** 1 July 2020 to 30 June 2025
- **Standard performance threshold:** at least 900 league minutes
- **Under-23 definition:** age 22 or younger at season end
- **Percentage-growth control:** starting market value of at least €1 million
- **Club attribution:** multi-club player-seasons were excluded where development attribution would be ambiguous
- **Positional comparisons:** medians were used to reduce distortion from elite-value outliers

> Market values are estimates rather than realised transfer fees. Growth should therefore be interpreted as changes in perceived market value, not guaranteed profit.

---

## Executive summary

**Jude Bellingham** recorded the largest absolute increase in market value across the project window, rising from **€27 million to €180 million**, an increase of **€153 million**. **Jamal Musiala** followed with a €139 million increase and also produced the strongest eligible percentage increase, rising from €1 million to €140 million.

Among under-23 player-seasons, **Lamine Yamal’s 2023–24 season** produced the largest absolute increase, at **€65 million**. Cole Palmer and Enzo Fernández followed with increases of €62 million and €60 million respectively.

At club level, **Brighton & Hove Albion** generated the strongest net value growth, at approximately **€330.6 million**, and developed the largest number of distinct high-growth players, with **14**. Eintracht Frankfurt and LOSC Lille ranked second and third for net value growth.

The Premier League held the highest total under-23 market value in four of the five seasons. The Bundesliga narrowly led in 2021–22. By 2024–25, the Premier League’s qualified under-23 group had a total value of **€1.189 billion** and the highest median value, at **€38 million**.

Attackers reached their highest recorded values at a median age of **24**, midfielders and defenders at **25**, and goalkeepers at **27**. This supports the expectation that goalkeeper market-value peaks tend to occur later.

The joint performance-and-value analysis identified **Randal Kolo Muani**, **Jude Bellingham**, **Désiré Doué**, **Victor Osimhen** and **Bukayo Saka** among the strongest cases where market-value growth coincided with improved attacking productivity. In contrast, the largest value declines were often associated with reduced playing time, with Sadio Mané and Douglas Luiz recording the two largest declines in the output.

# Methodology

## Full-window player growth

For Questions 1 and 2, each player’s earliest and latest eligible valuation within the project window were compared.

```text
Absolute growth = ending value − starting value
Percentage growth = absolute growth ÷ starting value × 100
```

## Player-season value growth

Valuations from July to December were assigned to the season beginning in that calendar year. Valuations from January to June were assigned to the previous season-start year.

The first and last valuation within each season were used for seasonal growth analysis.

## Club development

Only player-seasons involving one club were included. Club summaries distinguish:

- net value growth
- gross positive growth
- gross decline
- number of high-growth players

## Peak-value age

Each player’s highest recorded valuation was identified. Where the same peak value appeared more than once, the earliest date was used. Players required at least three valuation observations and a peak value of at least €1 million.

## Performance and valuation comparison

Consecutive seasons were compared using:

- change in ending market value
- change in league minutes
- change in goal contributions per 90

Both performance seasons required at least 900 minutes for the growth analysis.

---

# Main limitations

1. Market values are estimates rather than realised transfer prices.
2. The first and last valuations may not occur exactly at the beginning and end of a league season.
3. Market value can be influenced by age, contract duration, nationality, club reputation and transfer speculation.
4. Club-level value creation does not establish that the club caused the increase.
5. Multi-club seasons were excluded from club attribution, which removes some important transfer cases.
6. Percentage growth remains sensitive to starting values despite the €1 million threshold.
7. Goal contributions per 90 are incomplete measures for defenders and goalkeepers.
8. Peak-value age is based only on the project window and may not represent a player’s lifetime peak.
9. Declining minutes or productivity may coincide with value decline without causing it.
10. Currency values were analysed in nominal euros without inflation adjustment.
