# Theme 5 — Transfers and Post-Transfer Outcomes

## Business objective

Assess transfer-market flows, club recruitment strategies and player outcomes after changing clubs across Europe’s top five domestic leagues from 2020–21 to 2024–25.

This theme distinguishes between **positive known transfer fees**, **recorded zero-fee transfers**, and **missing or undisclosed fees**. Financial conclusions therefore refer to **known transfer expenditure, known transfer income and known-fee balances**, rather than complete transfer-market spending.

For post-transfer outcome analysis, the sample is restricted to **July–September transfers** to create cleaner before/after season comparisons. Sporting-outcome comparisons require at least **450 league minutes in both the pre-transfer and post-transfer seasons**.

---

## Executive summary

The transfer data shows a market in which known-fee financial activity is concentrated among a relatively small subset of transactions. Leicester recorded the highest known transfer expenditure in the club-level sample at **€117.0m**, while Aston Villa generated the highest known transfer income at **€86.05m**. Atalanta recorded the strongest positive known-fee net balance at **+€61.4m**, whereas Leicester recorded the largest negative balance at **−€105.15m**.

At league level, Serie A was the only top-five competition in the mapped sample to record a positive net player flow, with **143 incoming and 141 outgoing external transfers**. Ligue 1 recorded the largest net outflow at **−38 players**, followed by the Premier League at **−29**. Financially, the Premier League had the largest negative known external-transfer balance at approximately **−€159.1m**.

Transfer fees were highest in median terms for players aged **21–23 (€2.5m)** and for **midfielders (€2.5m)**. Goalkeepers had the lowest positional median fee at **€0.8m**.

Among clean summer-transfer cases with matched valuations, **Perr Schuurs** recorded the largest absolute post-transfer market-value increase at **+€18.0m**, while several lower-base players generated much larger percentage gains. Sporting outcomes were mixed: 57 non-goalkeeper/goalkeeper cases were classified as improved productivity or improved minutes and productivity, while 104 cases showed reduced productivity or declines in both minutes and productivity.

Young-player development outcomes highlighted **Torino**, **Atalanta**, and **UD Las Palmas**. When development was evaluated relative to known acquisition spending, **UD Las Palmas** ranked first, with net post-transfer value growth equal to approximately **1.88 times known spending**.

Among non-goalkeepers, the largest declines in goal contributions per 90 included Antonín Barák (−0.478), Francesco Caputo (−0.458), Andrea Petagna (−0.413), Marco Richter (−0.411), and Simone Bastoni (−0.406).

Across the eligible sporting-outcome cohort:

- 68 cases showed **declined minutes and productivity**

**Key finding:** Negative post-transfer outcomes were more common than positive ones in the final eligible sample. This should not be interpreted as transfer causation; the comparison only identifies changes following a move.

## Methodology notes

### Transfer-fee treatment

Transfer records were separated into three categories:

- **Positive known fee**
- **Recorded zero fee**
- **Missing or undisclosed fee**

Missing or undisclosed fees were never treated as zero. Club and league financial calculations therefore represent **known-fee activity only**.

### League mapping

Historical club-season league mappings were used where available, with current-club competition data used as a fallback. The final core transfer dataset had incomplete mapping coverage, especially on origin-club league assignments. For this reason, league-flow and route findings describe **mapped transfers**, not the complete transfer universe.

### Post-transfer outcome window

Post-transfer outcome analysis was restricted to transfers occurring from **July through September**. This avoids many winter-transfer cases where the same domestic season can include playing time for both the selling and buying club.

### Sporting-performance threshold

Questions 9 and 10 require at least **450 league minutes before and after the transfer**. This reduces noise from very small playing-time samples.

### Goalkeepers

Goals and assists are not treated as primary performance measures for goalkeepers. Goalkeeper post-transfer outcomes are interpreted mainly through changes in playing time.

### Market-value interpretation

Market-value changes are observational and should not be interpreted as causal effects of transfers. Changes may reflect age, team performance, contract status, injuries, league exposure and wider market conditions.

### Source-data limitations

Some source transfer records appear reciprocal, duplicated-looking or structurally unusual. These were retained unless clearly invalid because the raw source data was preserved rather than manually overwritten. Individual cases should therefore be checked before making player-specific claims.
